- [both] **Merge-block clamp face (unverified).** The small 1x1x1 merge block (`SmallShipSmallMergeBlock`) clamps toward its local **Up** face (one wiki source); the old assumption was Right. Every merge pair in this package is built on that. It is one constant (`MERGE_CLAMP_FACE_SMALL` in `tools/designs/_common.py`); if the merge-face test (T1) shows another face, change it and run `tools/build_all.py` again.
- [both] **Lite variants keep the small merge block.** Its XML shape comes from the Keen source, not from the Starburst reference, so strictly a Lite ship is not 100 % "seen" XML. A drone or printer without it cannot be printed or docked. `LITE_KEEPS_MERGE = False` in `_common.py` drops it from the drones.
- [both] **XML shapes never seen in a game-written blueprint:** Sensor, the AI blocks (Offensive, Flight, Defensive), Decoy, large gatling turret, Timer and Event Controller toolbars. They are why Full variants exist next to Lite ones. Earlier blueprints with a Sensor or Custom Turret Controller failed to paste; the leading hypothesis is a missing `<Toolbar>` element (now always written), but that is not confirmed.
- [both] **Projected Programmable Blocks.** A welded PB may keep its Program text but not start by itself. The printer script calls `ApplyAction("Recompile")` on it; whether that works is the projected-PB test (T5, T6).
- [both] **No ammunition is pre-loaded** in any gun or launcher (the generator cannot write inventories). In a survival world load magazines / rockets by hand; in a creative world the guns may still need magazines unless the world has infinite ammunition switched on (test T8 shows which). Scripts treat an unknown ammo count as unlimited and never return to base for lack of ammo in that case.
- [both] **Scripts have never run in the game with these versions.** They compile against a hand-written stub of the game API (`tools/compile_check.sh`), which proves syntax and types only.
- [both] **Weapon numbers** come from the local game data (gatling 700 rpm, 800 m/s, 800 m; small rocket 500 damage, 4 m blast; small railgun 1000 m/s, 1400 m, 500 ms shot delay). Rockets are assumed (v2.3) to leave with the shooter's velocity plus their launch speed, accelerate along the barrel and have their total speed capped, so a rocket fired from a moving ship curves onto the barrel line (Keen bug tracker). Test 18 in the test list checks it; if your rockets fly dead straight from a ship moving sideways, set `Rocket Velocity Model=NONE` on the Basilisk and Wasp.
- [drone] **Gnat / Midge Lite** thrust only along the nose axis (two axial thrusters, steered by gyros). They have no lateral or vertical thrusters by design.
- [drone] **Wasp** relies on the terminal action `ShootOnce` (name from memory, unverified). If it is wrong the Wasp never fires. Its target-lead solver was checked numerically only.
- [drone] **Midge (experimental)** has no script and no Programmable Block. The AI-block XML is a guess and its settings (attack pattern, selected gun, AI Behavior switch) cannot be pre-set: configure it by hand once, see `Scripts/MidgeNotes.md`. **Midge Lite** is only the Gnat Lite frame renamed and still runs `GnatSwarmAI.cs`.
- [drone] **Basilisk** is a large-grid ship with no merge block; it cannot be printed (paste only). Longbow S / L have no docking face in the base model (printers add a docking stalk to Longbow S and Mantis).
- [drone] Scripts that were forked from the Hornet script (Wasp, Lancer, Harrier, Aegis, Mantis, Basilisk, Watcher) do not share later bug fixes automatically. A drone only joins the hive of another model if both use the same `IGC Tag`.
- [printer] **Fused grid (biggest risk).** Blocks welded from a projection become part of the *projector's* grid. After switching both merge blocks off the drone may still be on the printer's grid. `HiveMotherAI` checks this at run time; if the drone is still on the printer's grid it sets the bay to FAULT with a "FUSED" message and sends no `_JOIN`. The fused-grid test (P1) decides. Test rig: Sprue Test (tests.md 45). Keen's old public source says a ship grinder skips blocks of its own grid (MyShipToolBase.CanInteractWithSelf is false, MyShipGrinder does not override it), so a grinder on the printer probably cannot cut a neck welded onto the printer (unverified in the current game; Sprue Test measures it). Game test 2026-10-14: a welded projected PB is on the printer's grid (same grid id as the printer PB), compiles by itself and keeps its Custom Data; its owner becomes the printer owner.
- [printer] **Welder reach is modelled, not measured:** the welder's work sphere centre comes from a model dummy we cannot read. v2.1 checks 8 guesses (centre at the tip face or 1 to 3 cells inside the welder, radius 1.3 m or 1.0 m); the outer hull is covered under all of them, deep core blocks (batteries, gyro, remote) only under the shallow ones. If the real centre is deeper, bays stall in FINISH with `N unfinished`, and the LCD lists which blocks.
- [printer] **Hornet-class drones need touching welders.** Hornet, Wasp, Lancer, Watcher, Harrier, Aegis, Mantis and Longbow S are 6 to 7 cells across, so their interior blocks can only be reached by welders whose tips touch the drone surface (a welder tip has no mount point, so no block attaches, but the surfaces do touch). If touching causes a permanent merge, use the Gnat-class printers (Hive Mother, Twin Hive, Quad Hive, Mobile Hive, Static Nest, Stinger Rack), which keep a full cell of air.
- [printer] **No conveyors.** The component stock must sit in a cargo container touching a welder's port, or in the welders themselves. A creative world may not need components at all (unconfirmed). The components needed per drone are listed as comment lines at the top of the PB Custom Data.
- [printer] **Cargo containers** (Full printers) have a guessed shape and volume; the Escort Carrier Full also has a guessed `SmallGatlingTurret`. Lite printers have neither.
- [printer] **Stinger Rack** prints a minimal fallback Stinger (Gnat frame, two warheads, `StingerAI.cs`) because there is no dedicated Stinger design module.
- [printer] Printers are heavy (about 13 to 36 t) and slow to turn; the Static Nest has no thrusters at all (it is still a dynamic grid). Timer / Event-Controller printers ("Midge Nest" and friends) are **not** part of this release.
- [both] **Power budget and run time (computed from `defs_stats.json`, not measured).** Hornet, Wasp, Lancer, Watcher, Harrier, Aegis: 4 small batteries = 0.2 MWh and at most 0.8 MW output; the 12 thrusters could draw 2.4 MW, so a burn on all three axes at once is capped at about 67 % thrust, and a one-axis burn (2 thrusters, 0.4 MW) lasts about 30 minutes. Gnat and Midge Lite: 1 battery = 0.05 MWh and 0.2 MW, i.e. about 15 minutes with the one thruster at full power. Longbow S and Mantis: one 3x2x3 battery = 1 MWh, 4 MW. Longbow L: 12 MWh, 48 MW against 40 MW of thrusters. Power drawn by guns, railgun charging, antenna range and welders is not in the game files, so it is not in these figures. There is no generator or solar panel on any drone: once the batteries are empty the drone is dead weight (`RTB Battery Fraction` sends it home before that).
- [both] **Space only.** Thrust is ion thrust with no gravity term: the best drones accelerate at 6 to 7 m/s2 (Harrier 12 m/s2 forward only), which is less than 1 g (9.81 m/s2), and ion thrusters lose most of their force in an atmosphere. None of these ships can lift off or hover over a planet. Use them in space or at the edge of low gravity. Masses are component masses only; magazines, rockets and cargo add weight.
- [printer] **A printed drone leaves with a nearly empty battery.** The CHARGE phase lasts at most `Max Charge Seconds` (default 25 s). A small battery charges at 0.2 MW, so 25 s adds only about 3 % of its 0.05 MWh; a full recharge of a Hornet's 0.2 MWh from the printer's 4 MW pack takes about 15 minutes. A freshly welded battery starts at its definition's initial charge (30 %, unverified for welded blocks), so expect a drone to leave with roughly one third of its endurance (about 10 minutes of one-axis burn for a Hornet class, 5 minutes for a Gnat). Raise `Max Charge Seconds` in the PB Custom Data if you can wait. Drones that return to the printer are not re-docked or recharged by the scripts. Keen's old public source: MyObjectBuilder_BatteryBlock.SetupForProjector zeroes the charge, then 30 % when functional (unverified in game); Sprue Test measures it.
- [printer] **PCU.** The table's PCU is the printer alone (welders cost 150 each). Every printed drone adds roughly 700 to 1,700 PCU, and every projected block counts 1 PCU while it is projected. A world with a low block limit (survival defaults) will refuse to paste a Quad Hive (8,381 PCU) or Static Nest (6,244 PCU).
- [drone] **AI Behavior switch from a script (unverified).** The Full drones' AI blocks paste with AI Behavior off. The scripts look for a bool terminal property whose id contains "Behavior" on each `[AI]` block and switch it on; the id is not documented (believed to be `ActivateBehavior`). The PB screen shows `AI Behavior on (N blocks)` when it found the switch, else `AI Behavior switch not found`: then switch it on by hand (see "AI blocks" above). Even switched on, the AI blocks keep their default settings (AI Flight speed limit 10 m/s, no weapon selected), which a blueprint cannot pre-set.
- [drone] **Camera-only search is weak.** Without working AI blocks (and on Lite variants) a drone finds enemies only with camera rays. In the simulator 3 Hornets with inert AI blocks found a pirate ship 700 m away in about 4 of 10 fights (was 1 in 40 before v2.1), mostly after it opened fire, and still lost most of those fights; with working AI blocks they found it within 3 s every time and won 3 of 4.
- [drone] **BREACH and SEEKER (v2.6) are sim-tested only.** BREACH assumes camera rays at a hull point come back deeper once blocks there die (true in the sim's block-exact raycast). SEEKER assumes `IMyLargeTurretBase.SetTargetingGroup("PowerSystems")` works from a script, that the game then aims the turret at a power block, and that `Vector3D.CreateFromAzimuthAndElevation(Azimuth, Elevation)` in the turret's frame is its barrel direction. Decoys pull the turret first. Test 23 checks all of it.
- [both] **Script size headroom is small.** Minified, the Basilisk script is 97,721 of the 100,000 characters a Programmable Block takes (Wasp 95,313, Mantis 94,623). The build stops with an error when a script goes over; the next big feature will need code moved out or trimmed.

- [ship] **One Helm script for all four ships.** `ShrikeAI.cs` (a Hornet-derived script, 79,990 of its 80,000 characters) flies the 60-tonne fighter and the 14-kilotonne dreadnought alike; the large ships differ only by Custom Data (stand-off 700 / 800 m, jinking off, `AI Block Search Assist` off, stand-off from the weapons off). A `HelmAI.cs` for the large ships was not needed in the sim; whether the game's gyro and thruster response lets the same controller settle on a 14 kt hull is in-game test 35. The script has no room left (10 characters): the next feature must remove something (the AI-block support is the agreed first cut).
- [ship] **Instruction counts.** The sim counts a block model (Helm peak about 1,700) and a pessimistic IL model (Helm peak about 25,000 of 50,000, Staff 9,500, bay 15,700, Hornet 17,000). Which of the two the game's counter resembles is not known (test 30); the integrity scan is sliced for that reason.
- [ship] **Large-ship fights are optimistic in the sim.** Large thrusters and batteries die 1.6 to 5.9 times too slowly there, hydrogen is unlimited, there is no conveyor model (ammunition and fuel are "free"), the enemy ships are stand-ins (the Pirate brain), and the connector-docked pods are held rigidly to the host (RigidLink: no real connector, no pod mass). Kill times and win rates in `docs/design/ships/*.md` are for comparing settings, not for predicting the game.
- [ship] **Small drones are hard to find at long range.** The Helm confirms what the AI blocks see with camera rays; a 54-block Hornet is 0.1 to 0.4 degrees wide at 0.5 to 1 km, so a heavy ship that turns slowly finds small attackers late (the Shrike with one forward camera lost to a single Hornet 4 times out of 5; with two forward cameras 2 out of 5). Against several Hornets a single Shrike always lost in the sim (the old M3 result for any single fighter). Use turret ships (Warden, Leviathan) or a wing against drone swarms.
- [ship] **Lite Shrike and Lite Corsair do not find targets on their own.** Lite has no AI blocks, no sensor and (Corsair) no turrets, so autonomous search is the camera sweep only: in the sim `Shrike Lite` and `Corsair Lite` lost 0/3 to the Pirate without firing a shot (the nose camera is shot off first, the sweep turns the ship at 1 rad/s and rarely hits). Fly them (`lock` on a target gives the Helm one), or use the Full ships. `Warden Lite` and `Leviathan Lite` keep their turrets and won 3/3 vs the Pirate (Warden Lite, 64-80 s).
- [ship] **Rotor turrets: nothing about them has run in the game (K-T1..K-T12; design record `docs/design/turrets.md` s8).** K-T1 whether the file pastes and the tops attach (the large chain is the Tech Hunter's own XML; the small-grid stators of the Shrike and Corsair are in no sample); K-T2 `TargetVelocityRad` sign against `Angle` (the script checks it at start and flips); K-T3 the rotors' velocity cap (30 / 60 rpm assumed: this sets the real turn rate); K-T4 jitter or "clang" at `Torque 1E+06` / `Braking Torque 1E+05`; K-T5 the small-grid top offsets (0.21 m, 0.166 m are the large ones / 5); K-T6 the hinge `Angle` range as a script reads it; K-T7 that the Helm leaves `[TU]` guns alone; K-T8 mask accuracy; K-T9 ammunition through a rotor or hinge (each gun holds only 4 magazines, 48 s of fire); K-T10 what the rotors do when the PB stops; K-T11 `GetTargetedEntity` velocity of a turret; K-T12 that bullets of a gun on a sub-grid inherit the ship's velocity (the lead assumes it). The simulator has no rotor model: no combat number exists. Rotor-elevation turrets (`TURRET_ELEVATION=rotor`, `docs/design/turrets.md` s11) add K-T13 the head built on a rotor's Top face pastes and attaches, K-T14 the `Angle` of an unlimited rotor, K-T15 `LowerLimitRad = float.MinValue` as no limit, K-T16 the small-grid elevation pair offset.
- [drone] **Tube ram damage is unmeasured.** The sim's collision damage (`CollisionDamageScale` 0.0005, symmetric, no deformation or penetration) is an assumption, and no tube has been pasted in the game. In-game test: paste a Tube 2x2 and a Tube 4x4, ram a pasted Basilisk and a Hornet at full speed (`lock`, then `launch`), report the blocks destroyed on each side, whether the tube survived and re-rammed, and the game log if anything fails to paste (`docs/design/tube_drones.md` Q6).
- [both] **`IMyCubeGrid.GetCubeBlock` returned null for plain armour blocks (observed, Sprue Test 2026-10-14).** 12 of 12 armour cells (the printer's stub and grinder stalk, the payload neck and hub) read null while battery, PB, thruster and welders were found at the same time. Scripts that scan armour with `GetCubeBlock` (HiveMotherAI FINISH scan, CradleBayAI lane check) are blind to it; `CubeExists` DOES see armour (second Sprue Test run: neck map `xxxxxxx` = GetCubeBlock null, CubeExists true for 7 of 7), so use it as the second opinion.
